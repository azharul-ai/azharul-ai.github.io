Why I’m sharing engineering notes

A portfolio shows what I have worked on. These notes will show how I think about building, improving, and maintaining software.

August 18, 2026 3 minute read By Md. Azharul Islam

Software engineering is full of lessons that are easy to forget once a ticket is closed or a release reaches production. I am creating this blog to keep the useful parts: the decisions, trade-offs, mistakes, and small improvements that can help another developer.

This will not be a collection of abstract definitions. My goal is to write practical notes that connect technical choices with the real work of delivering software.

What I plan to write about

The subjects will reflect the areas I work with and continue to learn:

  • Backend development with PHP, Laravel, C#, and .NET
  • API design, maintainable architecture, and integration work
  • Testing, delivery workflows, and debugging approaches
  • Responsible use of AI tools in software development
  • Communication, leadership, and career lessons for engineers

The publishing standard

Some drafts may begin with AI assistance, but every public article will be reviewed by me. I will check the technical claims, remove unsupported statements, and make sure the final writing reflects my own professional judgment.

The rule is simple: automation may prepare a draft, but it cannot approve the article.

I will also avoid publishing confidential company, client, or project information. Examples will be generalized or created specifically for teaching.

Why publish on my own site?

LinkedIn is useful for discovering ideas and starting conversations. This site gives those ideas a permanent home where longer explanations, examples, and updates can remain organized.

Over time, I hope these notes become a useful record of how my engineering approach develops—and a useful starting point for conversations with other people building software.
